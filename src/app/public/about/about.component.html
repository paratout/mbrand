About

Hello - I'm Mehdi.

I'm an enterprise architect based in Germany. I work with large organizations on the essential but often invisible question of how things fit together: business processes, application landscapes, data flows, and the governance that keeps them coherent.

Day to day, that means application portfolio management, business process architecture across functions like planning, purchasing, pricing, and supply chain, and helping teams make architecture decisions that hold up over time - not just on slides.

The road here

I did not start in architecture. I started in 2009 as a Linux and cloud administrator, back when "the cloud" still needed air quotes, and spent five years keeping infrastructure alive that preferred to break at night. Then I co-founded and ran an ERP integration company serving the manufacturing and steel industry for seven years - nothing teaches you what integration debt really costs like being the one who has to quote for fixing it.

Along the way I led software delivery teams in Germany on industrial and pharmaceutical projects, and moved into enterprise architecture proper: first in renewable energy, where I helped design and build a virtual power plant, and since then in large multi-country retail organizations - portfolios of several hundred applications, dozens of markets, and transformations that redraw the landscape while you are mapping it.

That mix - operations, entrepreneurship, delivery, then architecture - shapes how I work. I have been on the receiving end of bad governance too often to design any myself.

What I believe about the work

Governance should enable, not audit - if teams avoid your review, the review is the problem. Inventories and maps are promises an organization makes to itself, and they are only worth keeping if they are cheap to keep. Decisions belong in writing, where everyone can find them, or they will be happily re-litigated every quarter. And frameworks are tools, not religions - I am TOGAF certified and I will still pick the two-page version that people actually use over the binder every time.

This site is where I publish what I keep learning: enterprise architecture in practice, governance that enables rather than obstructs, and the realities of technology in large organizations. Everything in the library is free to take and adapt.
